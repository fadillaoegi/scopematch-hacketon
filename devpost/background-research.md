# ScopeMatch — Background Evidence (research notes)

Collected 15 Oct 2026. Working notes for scope/PRD; not submission text.
Status column: **verified** = read on the source page/abstract; **secondary** = seen only in
search summaries or third-party citations — check the primary before quoting.

## Evidence chain

1. **The same written requirement is read differently by different people.**
   - Yang, de Roeck, Gervasi, Willis & Nuseibeh (2011), "Analysing anaphoric ambiguity in natural
     language requirements", *Requirements Engineering* 16(3):163–189,
     doi:10.1007/s00766-011-0119-y. Defines *nocuous ambiguity*: text "interpreted differently by
     different readers". — **verified (abstract)**
   - arXiv 2607.04436 (2026): pragmatic ambiguity — stakeholders read a requirement differently
     depending on background (example: "real-time"). — **secondary**
   - Heidelberg ICSSEA 2000 paper: stakeholders often are not aware an ambiguity exists. —
     **secondary** (PDF link returned 404)

2. **Requirements problems are a leading cause of failed projects.**
   - PMI Pulse of the Profession, Requirements Management (2014): 47% of unsuccessful projects
     miss goals due to inaccurate requirements management. — **secondary** (pmi.org returned 403)
   - PMI Pulse 2018: 52% of projects had scope creep; 35% cite inaccurate requirements gathering.
     — **secondary**. Note: enterprise projects, not freelancers.

3. **Freelancers absorb the cost of unclear work.**
   - Freelancers Union nonpayment report (2015): 1 in 2 freelancers had payment trouble in 2014;
     71% at some point in career; avg $5,968 lost. — **secondary** (PDF not text-extractable here).
     Payment trouble ≠ scope misunderstanding; do not equate them.
   - UK creative freelancer survey (via Prolific North): 80% regularly work unpaid hours. —
     **secondary**; not tied to requirements.

## Gap (this is the honest framing)

No reliable freelancer-specific statistic isolates "client and freelancer read the brief
differently" as a cause. Research shows the ambiguity mechanism; surveys show freelancers pay
for unclear/unpaid work. ScopeMatch's own test should provide the missing link:
one real freelancer–client pair, answers recorded independently, differences counted.

## Do not use

- "67% of freelancers experienced scope creep (Freelancers Union 2025)" — could not confirm source.
- "57% lose $1,000–5,000/month" — blog citation of a vendor benchmark.
- BPS "pekerja bebas" (13.6M, Feb 2026) — includes casual/agricultural labor, not web freelancers.
- Any cost-saving or dispute-prevention claim for ScopeMatch itself (unmeasured).
